1. Waste Management
Area	Commitments (ESP 2022–24)	Reported Achievements & Status (ESR 2024 & Waste Guide)
Landfill Diversion	Divert at least 85% of general waste from landfill by 2025. Divert at least 90% of construction & demolition waste.	Not achieved: Achieved 82% landfill diversion in 2024 (down from 83% in 2021). Performance was impacted by regulatory changes requiring compostable food packaging to be directed to general waste/landfill rather than organic recycling streams.
General Waste Reduction	Reduce general waste weight by 20% compared to the 2018 baseline (target: 2,273 tonnes/year).	Not achieved: Total general waste reached 2,315 tonnes in 2024 due to increased campus activity. While total volume was 19% lower than 2018 levels (2,852t), it fell slightly short of the 20% target.
Plastic Free Dining	Ensure at least 80% of food & beverage retailers achieve Plastic Free Dining Gold Award status.	Not achieved: 62% of retailers (16 of 26) achieved Gold status. However, 100% of campus retailers achieved either a Gold or Silver award, reflecting progress in phasing out single-use plastics.
Downstream Processing	Maintain & implement the Waste Management Plan.	Detailed operational execution: General waste sent to Doyle Bros MRF yields 35% paper/cardboard recycling, 4% metal recovery, 3% plastic recovery, and 42% Processed Engineered Fuel (PEF), leaving only 16% of general bin weight in landfills [193–195]. Food organics are processed via anaerobic digestion at EarthPower to produce electricity and soil fertilizer.

2. Energy Use & Climate Action
Area	Commitments (ESP 2022–24)	Reported Achievements & Status (ESR 2024)
Operational Net Zero	Maintain net zero operational (Scope 1 and 2) emissions.	Achieved: Maintained 0 tCO2e net operational emissions since 2020 through renewable power purchases, efficiency measures, LGC surrenders, and carbon offset purchases.
100% Renewable Electricity	Procure 100% renewable electricity via solar Power Purchase Agreement (PPA).	Achieved: 100% solar renewable electricity maintained continuously across operational sites.
Onsite Solar Capacity	Expand onsite solar PV capacity to 1.5 MWp by 2025.	Achieved: Expanded capacity to 1.53 MWp in 2024 following new PV installations on the Robert Webster Building, Manly Vale campus, and David Phillips Field.
Total Emissions Reduction	Reduce total value chain (Scope 1, 2, and 3) emissions on a 1.5°C pathway: 30% by 2025, 50% by 2030, and net zero by 2050.	Achieved early: Total 2024 emissions dropped to 215,517 tCO2e, representing a 32% reduction against the 2018 baseline (317,278 tCO2e), exceeding the 2025 milestone a year early.
Electrification Program	Implement Stage 1 of the Electrification Strategy to phase out natural gas and fossil fuel equipment.	Achieved: Stage 1 completed across Paddington Campus, David Phillips Fields, Goldstein Hall kitchen, and Kensington Colleges, replacing gas systems with electric heat pumps, induction cooktops, and dryers [69, 79, 81–82].
Energy Intensity	Reduce energy intensity by 5% against 2018 baseline (from 199 to 189 kWh/m²/year).	Not achieved: Energy intensity increased to 210 kWh/m²/year in 2024 due to higher overall electricity and natural gas consumption across campus facilities.

3. Responsibilities & Governance
Institutional & Executive Governance
•	Strategic Oversight: The Vice-President Operations serves as Senior Sponsor. Overall coordination is led by the Head of Environmental Sustainability within Estate Management, while approval and monitoring are governed by the University Council, Management Board, and Estates Portfolio Board [7, 38, 42–43, 60].
•	Operational Accountability: Specific directors hold direct responsibility for key targets—the Director Facilities Management oversees electrification, renewable energy procurement, and waste/energy action plans; the Director Treasury, Investment & Insurance manages fossil fuel divestment; and the Director Procure to Pay implements the Sustainable Procurement Framework.
Staff & Student Responsibilities
•	Source-Level Waste Sorting: Staff and students carry direct responsibility for sorting waste accurately across color-coded campus bins (Red, Yellow, Blue, Green, White Smart Bins) to avoid contaminating recyclable and compostable streams [184–190].
•	Reuse & Waste Avoidance: Community members are expected to adopt personal reusables (BYO cups and containers), participate in office furniture reuse programs, and engage in campus-wide lab swap events.
•	Laboratory Sustainability (LEAF): Research staff and lab teams are responsible for implementing the Laboratory Efficiency Assessment Framework (LEAF)—with 59 lab teams accredited across 40% of UNSW lab spaces to minimize single-use plastics, chemical waste, and energy usage [126–129, 157].
•	Active Engagement: Staff and students participate in Faculty Sustainability Working Groups, Climate Fresk science workshops, Arc Go Green, and UNSW Urban Growers to embed sustainable habits across campus.
