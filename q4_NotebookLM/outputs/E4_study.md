Question 1: Climate Action Targets & Emissions Progress
Question: What are UNSW’s total (Scope 1, 2, and 3) greenhouse gas emissions reduction targets, and what performance milestone was achieved in 2024 against the 2018 baseline?
Answer:
•	Targets: UNSW committed to reducing its total value chain emissions (Scope 1, 2, and 3) on a 1.5°C Paris Agreement pathway by 30% by 2025, 50% by 2030, and reaching net zero emissions by 2050 compared to the 2018 baseline.
•	2024 Performance: Total calendar year emissions dropped to 215,517 tCO2e, representing a 32% reduction against the 2018 baseline (317,278 tCO2e). Because emissions fell below the 331,069 tCO2e target threshold, UNSW achieved its 2025 milestone a year early.

Question 2: Electrification Program Milestones
Question: What major milestone was completed under Stage 1 of UNSW's Electrification Program, and what specific gas-powered appliances were replaced across the Kensington Colleges?
Answer:
•	Milestone: UNSW completed Stage 1 of the Electrification Program, fully electrifying Paddington Campus, David Phillips Fields, and seven key buildings on Kensington Campus (including all five Kensington Colleges).
•	Equipment Replaced: Gas-powered systems were replaced with all-electric alternatives powered by 100% renewable electricity, including:
o	7 domestic hot water heat pumps
o	34 heat pump-operated dryers and 34 high-efficiency washers
o	31 induction cooktops and 10 electric barbecues
o	High-efficiency commercial electric fryers, pressurized bratt pans, combi ovens, and induction cooktops in the Goldstein Hall kitchen, which serves over 1,300 daily meals.

Question 3: Waste Management Commitments vs. Outcomes
Question: What were UNSW's general waste targets for landfill diversion and total weight reduction, and what specific constraints prevented these targets from being achieved in 2024?
Answer:
•	Commitments: Divert at least 85% of general waste from landfill by 2025 and reduce total general waste weight by 20% against the 2018 baseline (targeting 2,273 tonnes/year).
•	Outcomes & Constraints:
o	Landfill Diversion (82% Achieved): Missed the 85% target due to updated state regulations that required compostable food packaging and cups to be directed to general waste/landfill rather than organic processing streams.
o	General Waste Reduction (19% Achieved): Total general waste reached 2,315 tonnes in 2024. While this was 19% below 2018 levels (2,852 tonnes), increased campus population and operational activity prevented reaching the full 20% reduction mark.

Question 4: Campus Bin Sorting Guidelines
Question: According to the UNSW Waste Guide, in which bin should soft plastics, coffee cups, and takeaway food containers be disposed of? What basic rule applies if a student or staff member is unsure where an item belongs?
Answer:
•	Correct Bin Placement: Takeaway food containers (including paper/bamboo compostable ones), coffee cups and lids, cutlery, chopsticks, paper towels/tissues, and soft plastics (wrappers, plastic bags, wrap) must be disposed of in the General Waste (Red) Bin.
•	Uncertainty Rule: If staff or students are unsure which bin to use, they are advised to place the item in the Red Bin to avoid contaminating organic, paper, or yellow bin recycling streams.

Question 5: Laboratory Waste Reduction Initiatives
Question: How does the Laboratory Efficiency Assessment Framework (LEAF) promote research sustainability, and what impact did the 2024 inaugural lab swap events have on lab consumables and carbon emissions?
Answer:
•	LEAF Program: LEAF is an international accreditation framework supporting lab teams to reduce single-use plastic, energy, water, and chemical waste while improving research quality. By the end of 2024, 59 lab teams across 40% of UNSW lab spaces were accredited (35 bronze, 18 silver, and 6 gold awards).
•	Lab Swap Events: Organized by the Technet committee in August and December 2024, lab swap events allowed labs to donate and claim unused equipment and consumables (such as pipettes, tubes, and 3D printers). The inaugural event redistributed ~$26,000 worth of lab supplies (nearly 80 boxes of consumables and 7 equipment items), diverting over 70% of donated supplies from waste streams and avoiding ~12 tonnes of CO2e emissions.
