UNSW’s sustainability commitments are framed around its overall strategic vision—Progress for All (specifically Pillar 6: "Accelerate the transition to a sustainable society and planet")—and its Environmental Sustainability Plan 2022–24. The university aligns its operational and academic goals with seven United Nations Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs 4, 6, 7, 11, 12, 13, and 15) [17, 62–65] across three core themes:
1. Climate Action
•	Net Zero Science-Based Pathway: Reduce total value chain (Scope 1, 2, and 3) greenhouse gas emissions on a 1.5°C Paris Agreement pathway by 30% by 2025, 50% by 2030, and achieve net zero emissions by 2050 compared to the 2018 baseline. (The 2025 milestone was achieved early in 2024 with a 32% reduction).
•	Net Zero Operational Emissions: Maintain net zero operational (Scope 1 and 2) emissions.
•	Renewables & Electrification: Procure 100% renewable electricity, expand onsite solar PV capacity to 1.5 MWp (achieved 1.53 MWp in 2024), and implement the Electrification Strategy to phase out natural gas and fossil fuels [9, 10, 14, 25, 81–83].
•	Fossil Fuel Divestment: Fully divest investments in listed companies that derive more than 20% of their revenue from fossil fuel reserves by December 2025.
2. Living Campuses
•	Biodiversity & Ecosystem Conservation: Achieve a net gain in biodiversity value on the Kensington campus relative to the baseline and establish the 39,000-hectare Fowlers Gap Arid Zone Research Station as a dedicated conservation reserve.
•	Sustainable Capital Projects: Ensure all new buildings and refurbishments meet strict minimum sustainability standards, including a minimum 5 Green Star Buildings rating, 5.5 NABERS Energy/Water ratings, all-electric designs, and a minimum 30% reduction in embodied carbon.
•	Sustainable Transport: Ensure at least 85% of students and staff travel to campus using sustainable modes of travel (such as public transport, walking, or cycling).
•	Education & Community Engagement: Promote environmental awareness across campus through academic toolkits, interactive climate workshops like Climate Fresk, and expanding the Laboratory Efficiency Assessment Framework (LEAF) across research labs.
3. Resource Efficiency
•	Energy & Water Reduction: Reduce both energy intensity and water intensity by 5% compared to 2018 baselines. (Water intensity dropped significantly, while energy intensity faced operational challenges).
•	Waste Reduction & Landfill Diversion: Divert at least 85% of general waste from landfill and reduce total general waste weight by 20% against 2018 levels.
•	Elimination of Single-Use Plastics: Ensure at least 80% of food and beverage retailers on campus achieve Plastic Free Dining Gold Award status.
•	Sustainable Procurement & Operations: Embed the Sustainable Procurement Framework across supply chains, establish Green Cleaning Standards, and maintain segregated waste recovery systems [185, 187, 188, 190, 193–196].
